Motivation
Memorising complex information (vocabulary, historical events, scientific concepts) is a persistent challenge. People routinely underutilise the brain's greatest strength: spatial & contextual memory.

Studies show recall improvement of 5–9% when spatial placement is used versus flat-list study. Yet most digital learning tools offer only passive content review, leaving the core power of the technique untapped.
Method of Loci
Traced to ancient Greek orators (Cicero, De Oratore, 55 BC), the technique associates each item to memorise with a distinct location along a mentally traversed path.
"The method relates what we want to learn with a story and a real or imagined location, the chambers of our imagined palace." - Bierig & Krueger, QoMEx 2020
Episodic spatial memory (hippocampal) has far greater fidelity than semantic list memory. MENTIS externalises the internal palace into a navigable 3D space.
Research Questions
  1. 1Can a browser-based 3D environment serve as an effective locus system for diverse learners without VR hardware?
  2. 2Does active object placement outperform passive content review in recall tasks?
  3. 3How usable is the system for first-time users, measured by SUS score and qualitative observation?

Active Learning Paradigm
Users receive a learning topic (e.g. Nobel Prize winners) and construct a personal story by placing objects through rooms. Active encoding (choosing, placing, composing objects) drives deeper processing than passive reading, consistent with the generation effect in cognitive psychology.
To remember "James Chadwick, discovery of the neutron": place a top hat (Sir) next to a small atom model in the study. A vivid, location-anchored scene, unforgettable.

Key Features
  • 3D Room Navigation: orbit, pan, inspect each locus from any angle
  • Spatial Note Anchors: attach mnemonic text tags to precise 3D positions
  • GLB/GLTF Upload: import custom 3D models or use built-in props
  • Sketchfab Integration: browse thousands of community 3D objects
  • Room Management: create, rename, sequence multiple palace rooms
  • Session Persistence: layouts saved server-side, accessible across visits
  • No Headset Required: runs entirely in a browser, XR optional
The Virtual House (Loci)
Designed per established memory palace guidelines: familiar, ordered, emptied, distinct, and rendered in a low-poly caricaturist style that preserves salience without visual overload.

Conclusion
MENTIS demonstrates that the ancient method of loci can be faithfully implemented in a modern web browser with no specialist hardware. By combining a navigable low-poly 3D house, interactive spatial anchor placement, and persistent session memory, the system delivers the full generative power of the memory palace. Above-average SUS scores validate the interface; qualitative data confirms learner engagement and creative drive.
